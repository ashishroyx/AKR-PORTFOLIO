I didn’t start with some grand plan. I wasn’t born writing code on scraps of paper or hacking into things at 12. I was just curious. That curiosity mixed with frustration, late nights, and a lot of “why the hell is this not working” pulled me into the world of coding.  

Like most people, I started with the basics: HTML, CSS, a little JavaScript. At first, it felt like magic. Just a few lines and suddenly there’s something on the screen that I made. But soon enough, I realized it wasn’t magic at all. It was patience, trial and error, and a lot of Googling.  

I broke things. A lot. And honestly, that became my way of learning. Break, fix, repeat. It wasn’t always pretty, but every bug I fixed and every tiny feature I built gave me this rush, like I was leveling up one line of code at a time.  

Over time, I moved from simple pages to building real apps. React became my main playground, and Tailwind gave me a way to make things look good without drowning in endless CSS. Then came the backend with Flask, Express, and MongoDB, and I started connecting the dots between how data flows, how APIs work, and how the frontend and backend actually shake hands.  

But here’s the truth: it’s not just about the tech stack. It’s about the process. About the times I stared at my screen, wondering if I was cut out for this. About the nights where the only thing that kept me going was the thought that if I just pushed a little more, I would figure it out.  

Through it all, one thing has stayed constant. I’m still chasing mastery. Not perfection, because that doesn’t exist in code, but growth. That thrill of learning something new, of turning an idea into reality, of realizing that the thing I once thought was impossible is now running on my localhost.  

This is me. Not a prodigy, not a genius, just someone who chose to keep showing up, breaking things, and building again. If you’ve ever felt stuck, frustrated, or unsure on your coding journey, know this: you’re not alone. We’ve all been there. And every bug you solve is proof that you’re moving forward.  

I’m still learning, still breaking, still building. And honestly, I wouldn’t have it any other way.
